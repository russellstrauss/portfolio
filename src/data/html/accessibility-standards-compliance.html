Accessibility Standards & Compliance

Understanding and adhering to established accessibility standards is crucial for creating inclusive digital experiences and ensuring legal compliance. This guide covers the primary standards and regulations that govern web accessibility.

WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines)

The WCAG, developed by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C), is the internationally recognized standard for web accessibility. The current version, WCAG 2.1, provides guidelines organized into three conformance levels:

Level A (Minimum)

The most basic level of accessibility. Meeting Level A ensures that the most fundamental accessibility features are present. Examples include:

  • Providing alt text for images
  • Ensuring keyboard navigation is possible
  • Using proper heading structure
  • Providing labels for form inputs

Level AA (Recommended)

This is the standard most organizations aim for, as it addresses the major barriers for users with disabilities. Level AA requirements include:

  • Color contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text
  • Text can be resized up to 200% without loss of functionality
  • Multiple ways to find pages (navigation, search, sitemap)
  • Consistent navigation and identification
  • Error identification and suggestions for correction

Level AAA (Enhanced)

The highest level of accessibility, often difficult to achieve for entire sites but valuable for specific content. Includes:

  • Sign language interpretation for audio content
  • Extended audio descriptions
  • No timing constraints
  • Enhanced color contrast (7:1 for normal text)

Legal Requirements

Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)

In the United States, the ADA requires that public accommodations, including websites, be accessible to people with disabilities. While the ADA doesn't specify technical standards, courts have consistently referenced WCAG 2.0 Level AA as the benchmark for compliance.

Section 508

Federal agencies and organizations receiving federal funding must comply with Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act, which requires adherence to WCAG 2.0 Level AA standards.

European Accessibility Act (EAA)

EU member states require certain products and services, including websites and mobile applications, to meet accessibility requirements based on EN 301 549, which aligns with WCAG 2.1 Level AA.

Compliance Best Practices

  • Regular Audits: Conduct accessibility audits using automated tools and manual testing
  • User Testing: Include people with disabilities in your testing process
  • Documentation: Maintain an accessibility statement and documentation of compliance efforts
  • Training: Ensure development and design teams are trained on accessibility standards
  • Continuous Monitoring: Implement accessibility checks in your development workflow

WCAG 2.2 Updates

WCAG 2.2, released in 2023, adds new success criteria while maintaining backward compatibility with WCAG 2.1. New requirements focus on:

  • Focus not obscured (minimum)
  • Dragging movements
  • Target size (minimum)
  • Fixed reference points
  • Accessible authentication

Organizations should plan to meet WCAG 2.2 Level AA standards to ensure future compliance and improved accessibility.
