Accessibility in Design & UX

Accessibility should be considered from the earliest stages of design. Inclusive design benefits all users and creates better user experiences. This guide covers accessibility considerations for designers and UX professionals.

Designing for All Users

Universal Design Principles

  • Equitable Use: Design is useful to people with diverse abilities
  • Flexibility in Use: Accommodates a wide range of preferences and abilities
  • Simple and Intuitive: Easy to understand regardless of experience or language
  • Perceptible Information: Communicates effectively regardless of ambient conditions or user abilities
  • Tolerance for Error: Minimizes hazards and adverse consequences of accidental actions
  • Low Physical Effort: Can be used efficiently and comfortably
  • Size and Space for Approach and Use: Appropriate size and space for approach and use

Visual Design Considerations

Color and Contrast

  • Maintain WCAG AA contrast ratios (4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text)
  • Never rely on color alone to convey information
  • Test designs with color blindness simulators
  • Provide alternative indicators (icons, patterns, text labels)
  • Ensure focus indicators are visible against all backgrounds
  • Consider dark mode and high contrast mode compatibility

Typography

  • Choose readable fonts (avoid overly decorative fonts for body text)
  • Ensure sufficient font size (minimum 16px for body text)
  • Maintain adequate line height (1.5x font size minimum)
  • Limit line length (50-75 characters ideal)
  • Use proper heading hierarchy visually
  • Ensure text can be resized up to 200% without breaking layout

Spacing and Layout

  • Provide adequate white space for clarity
  • Ensure touch targets are at least 44x44 pixels
  • Maintain consistent spacing patterns
  • Design for content reflow at different screen sizes
  • Avoid fixed-width layouts that break at zoom
  • Ensure interactive elements have sufficient spacing

Information Architecture

Clear Navigation

  • Provide multiple ways to find content (navigation, search, sitemap)
  • Use consistent navigation patterns
  • Provide clear labels and descriptions
  • Indicate current location (breadcrumbs, active states)
  • Limit navigation depth when possible
  • Ensure navigation is keyboard accessible

Content Organization

  • Use clear, descriptive headings
  • Group related content together
  • Provide clear page structure
  • Use lists for list content
  • Break up long content with headings
  • Provide summaries or overviews for complex content

Interaction Design

Form Design

  • Place labels above or to the left of inputs
  • Group related fields visually and semantically
  • Provide clear required field indicators
  • Show helpful placeholder text (but don't rely on it)
  • Design clear error states
  • Provide inline validation feedback
  • Ensure forms are logical and not overwhelming

Feedback and States

  • Provide clear visual feedback for all interactions
  • Design distinct states (default, hover, focus, active, disabled)
  • Ensure focus indicators are highly visible
  • Provide loading states for async operations
  • Design clear success and error messages
  • Use animations purposefully and allow users to disable

Error Prevention and Recovery

  • Design to prevent errors before they occur
  • Provide clear confirmation for destructive actions
  • Allow users to undo actions when possible
  • Design clear, helpful error messages
  • Provide suggestions for error correction
  • Ensure users can easily recover from mistakes

Content Design

Writing for Accessibility

  • Use clear, simple language
  • Write descriptive link text (avoid "click here")
  • Provide context for abbreviations and acronyms
  • Use plain language for instructions
  • Break up long paragraphs
  • Use lists for sequential or related items
  • Provide summaries for complex content

Alternative Text Strategy

  • Work with content creators to write meaningful alt text
  • Provide guidelines for when images are decorative vs. informative
  • Consider context when writing alt text
  • Ensure alt text conveys the same information as the image
  • Keep alt text concise but descriptive

Responsive and Flexible Design

Mobile-First Accessibility

  • Design for touch targets (44x44px minimum)
  • Ensure content is accessible on small screens
  • Test navigation patterns on mobile
  • Consider thumb-friendly interaction zones
  • Ensure forms work well on mobile devices

Scalable Design

  • Design with relative units (em, rem, %) in mind
  • Ensure layouts work at 200% zoom
  • Test designs at different font sizes
  • Avoid fixed pixel dimensions for text
  • Design flexible, fluid layouts

Multimedia Design

Video and Audio

  • Plan for captions in video designs
  • Ensure captions don't obscure important visual information
  • Design accessible media players
  • Provide transcripts for audio content
  • Consider audio descriptions for video
  • Don't auto-play audio or video

Design Systems and Component Libraries

Accessible Design Tokens

  • Define color contrast ratios in design tokens
  • Establish spacing scales that work for accessibility
  • Define typography scales with accessibility in mind
  • Document focus indicator styles
  • Create accessible component patterns

Component Documentation

  • Document accessibility features of each component
  • Provide keyboard interaction patterns
  • Include ARIA usage guidelines
  • Show examples of proper implementation
  • Document known limitations

Design Review Checklist

  • Color contrast meets WCAG AA standards
  • Information is not conveyed by color alone
  • Focus indicators are visible and clear
  • Touch targets are at least 44x44 pixels
  • Text is readable and can be resized
  • Navigation is clear and consistent
  • Forms are well-organized and labeled
  • Error states are clear and helpful
  • Content structure is logical
  • Design works at different zoom levels

Collaboration with Developers

  • Provide design specifications that include accessibility requirements
  • Communicate interaction patterns clearly
  • Discuss focus management for complex components
  • Share design rationale for accessibility decisions
  • Review implemented designs for accessibility
  • Work together to solve accessibility challenges

User Research and Testing

  • Include people with disabilities in user research
  • Test designs with assistive technologies
  • Gather feedback on accessibility barriers
  • Iterate based on accessibility findings
  • Document accessibility insights
